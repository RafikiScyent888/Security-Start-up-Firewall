The spine, start to finish

4 of 6 Tier 1 — The house · The four-pack

Somebody bought four cameras in one box and wanted to watch them from work.

What actually happened. The Front Door Camera was taken at 20:08:00 on day 1. Somebody reached it from the internet and logged in with a credential they did not have to guess. It has been talking to an address no device in this house had ever used since.

Why there was a difference. The hole is shut now, and it is still talking. Closing a hole stops the next arrival and does nothing about the one already inside.

How it was handled.

The good

  • The cameras cannot reach the family's devices. That does not stop one being taken — it stops a taken one being useful, which is the whole of what segmentation buys.
  • You opened the log. Nothing in this house was ever going to interrupt you, and the only reason you know anything at all is that you went and looked.
  • You worked it out by elimination — 2 ruled out with a reason before you settled. That is the job; the board was doing the remembering so you did not have to.

The bad

  • You closed the way in, and you closed it late. It stops the next arrival and does nothing about the one already inside — which is the difference between containment and prevention, and it is the most expensive sentence in this tier.

The ugly

  • 6 devices are still on credentials that are published or reused. Fixing one of a set changes nothing when they all share it.

What this costs in the real world

None of this is your balance — there is no business yet. These are the ordinary numbers for a firm of the size you are about to build, so that later on, when something is too expensive, you know what too expensive means.

  • What a small managed-service firm bills. Break-fix work runs roughly $95–150 an hour. Managed contracts run a few thousand a month for a ten-to-thirty device client. Five clients of that size is somewhere near $10,000 a month.
  • Where the money actually is. Hardware resale turns over the largest number and keeps the smallest slice — thirty laptops bought at $1,200 and sold at $1,350 is $40,500 of revenue and $4,500 of margin. Reselling cloud is similar. The recurring managed work is the smallest number on the page and the profitable one, which surprises nearly everybody.
  • Staff. The rule of thumb is $150–200k of revenue per employee. That is why a twelve-person firm cannot be run on $120,000 a year, and why the business has to grow before the headcount can.
  • What incidents cost. A business email compromise of the ordinary kind takes $20–40k out of somebody. Outside forensics is $10–30k, breach counsel $5–15k. A regulated breach at a firm this size reaches six figures once notification and a settlement are in.
  • What the controls cost. The ones that would have stopped most of the above cost nothing: turning on an alert, changing a password, closing a port, reading a log. The expensive controls are rarely the ones that save you.
